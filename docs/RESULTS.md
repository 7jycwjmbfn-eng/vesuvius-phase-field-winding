# Results and experiments

All numbers come from the scripts in `src/`, run on the dates in the logs of the working directory. Areas are in cm2 (L2 voxel edge 9.6 um). The ruler is `src/surfkit_8c.py jump` through `src/eval_surfs.py`: human-verified patch samples, window L3 z 5248-5504, patch samples inside L3 y 576-1792 and x 896-2432. Switch rate = switch samples / on-patch samples ("piece" mode, one surface id per connected grid component).

Terms. A relative-winding annotation is a point collection in which numbered points give the number of windings between them (the files `relative_windings.json` of the public spiral dataset; the PCU report calls such a line a ladder). A generated constraint is a point collection of the same kind written by this code. The segment-label field is the winding label derived from the official segments. Names without an official equivalent are this project's own: winding-count network (a network that outputs the number of windings between two points), combined certifier (the winding-count network and a gradient-boosting certifier combined by a further gradient-boosting model, a stacking step), strict subset (generated constraints kept by link probability and confidence).

## Boxes

Box A: L2 z 10496-11008, y 1920-4992, x 3328-6400 (crop L3 y 960-2496, x 1664-3200). Box B: L2 z 10496-11008, y 640-3712, x 1792-4864 (crop L3 y 320-1856, x 896-2432). Both are centred on the umbilicus region of Paris 4. The network was trained on L2 z 10000-10380 in the columns of the W2 box (y 2304-3840, x 3584-5120, labels from the official segments); z 10500 and above was not seen in training, so a box is held out in z, and in x and y outside W2, while the W2 slab itself is held out in z only. The check region of box A (L2 y 1920-3584, x 3328-4864) lies inside that of box B. About 64% of the area of the box A check region and about 22% of the box B check region lie inside the W2 columns (y 2304-3840, x 3584-5120), and the development slab below (the lower half of the box A window, 1526 of the 3085 box A on-patch samples, the first count without joins) lies in the W2 columns. The two boxes are therefore not independent replications. The percentages are computed from the coordinates and are approximate.

## Global min-cut integer labelling (exploration, not adopted)

Replacing the tile-offset sync by a global minimum-cut labelling (PUMA graph cuts, confidence-weighted, block-parallel with a checkerboard schedule) lowered the cut cost by a factor of 3.8 on the development slab and added 20% to 25% of pure and clean area at the same patch check switch rate (0.6%). On box A it raised the patch check switch rate from 1.8% to 10.1% (area-weighted purity against the winding field 89.6% to 84.0%) with 24% more clean area, no joins in both cases. The criteria were written before the readings (`docs/lab_notes/PREREG_bigbox_cn.md`), and the result fails both purity criteria on box A.

Variants that trade switch rate for 20% to 25% more area, all measured with the same patch check: direct wrapped surfaces, wrapped surfaces with label-based bridge cutting, carrier-compensated complex filtering (switch 2.4% to 5.7% on the development slab against 0.6%), erosion 0 with the gate, graph-cut labelling.

## Winding-count certificates with path votes and a learned certifier for mixed pair types (all 256 annotation collections)

`src/ladder_run3.py` adds two more phase counts (the v6 phase integrated along minimum-cost paths that avoid low-confidence cores, cost 1/conf^2 and 1/conf^4), the surface-prediction crossings on the path, the number of peaks of the smoothed CT profile and the grad_mag sum on the path. Pairs with a straight-line length above 120 voxels or a path crop above 4e5 voxels have no path votes. 17,425 pairs in total; the network's training z range (L2 9984-10500) is excluded in the numbers below, which leaves 16,730 pairs after the regions that touch the truth box (19 of 256) are also removed for the learned certifier. Of the 256 collections, 16 lose all their pairs to the z exclusion and 13 more to the removal of the touching regions (6 of those 19 regions had already lost all pairs), which leaves 227 collections. `src/ladder_certify3.py` (single votes and rules), `src/ladder_tables.py` (the learned certifier tables below; log `ladder_tables.log`).

Single votes on one-winding pairs: v6 84.3%, flip-y 85.4%, flip-x 85.4%, frozen_8c 83.8%, v4 85.0%, parallel lines 84.5% and 84.0%, path (1/conf^2) 85.3%, path (1/conf^4) 87.1%, grad_mag straight 84.6%, grad_mag on the path 87.8%, cos peaks 68.7%, CT peaks 56.4%.

Rule "certified count = grad_mag count, accepted when at least k of the 9 phase votes equal it", one-winding pairs: k >= 1: coverage 80.8%, precision 99.1% (n=1520, 98.5 to 99.5); k >= 9: 65.1%, 99.3% (n=1226). Seven of the nine phase votes come from the output of the same network (v6 itself, flipped inputs, two parallel lines, two paths), so these counts are separately computed and partly correlated.

The learned certifier (gradient boosting on the vote pattern, the confidences and the pair length; 5 folds by collection) is trained and evaluated on pairs of 1, 2, 3 and 4 windings together with ONE threshold: 5,943 pairs (1810, 1583, 1364, 1186) from 227 collections. An earlier version was fitted on one-winding pairs only and certified 5.5% of two-winding pairs as one winding; the numbers here replace it. The 10,787 pairs of five or more windings are neither used nor scored; at the 99% threshold 36 of them are certified with a count of 1 to 4 (1.6% of the 2,307 certified pairs), so the stated precision holds for pairs whose true count is 1 to 4. The grad_mag integral is turned into a count with one slope per vote that is fitted inside the training folds only (a slope fitted on all pairs raises the coverage at 99% from 38.8% to 44.2%, because the slope sees the truth of the test fold). The threshold is set on the out-of-fold predictions for the stated overall precision, so the precision is the target and the coverage is the measurement. Intervals in brackets are 95% bootstrap intervals over collections (500 resamples).

The later sections (`compare_ladder.py`, `stack_ladder.py`; logs `compare_run2.log`, `stack_run2_full.log`) use the same folds, slopes and pairs but set the threshold of each fold on the other four folds. That rule gives, for the same gradient-boosting certifier, a total coverage of 38.1% (precision 99.03%) at the 99% target, 51.7% (98.05%) at 98% and 21.9% (99.31%) at 99.5%. At the 99% target by pair type it gives one winding 67.1%, two windings 32.4%, three windings 21.9% and four windings 19.9%, against 67.3%, 33.4%, 22.5% and 21.4% here; for the bands, 8400-9400 64.1% (100%) and 11000-12000 65.6% (100%), against 64% (100%) and 66% (99.4%) here. One-winding pairs by z band at the 99% operating point, coverage and precision of the certified pairs: L2 z below 8000: 67%, 100% (n=43, 6 collections); 8000-10000: 63%, 100% (n=547, 48 collections); 8400-9400: 64%, 100% (n=468, 41); 10000-12000: 68%, 98.6% (n=320, 62 collections, bootstrap 96.7 to 100); 11000-12000: 66%, 99.4% (n=247, 47 collections, bootstrap 97.7 to 100); 12000-14000: 72%, 100% (n=289); 14000-16000: 68%, 100% (n=526); above 16000: 72%, 100% (n=85, 5 collections). The intervals of the single-vote and rule rows above are Wilson intervals that treat pairs as independent; pairs of one collection share points, so they are too narrow.

The grad_mag integral in the lasagna output is stored as uint8 with a mean of about 100 (0.1 per voxel after our scaling), so its line integral is close to length divided by sheet spacing plus the sheet peaks. It works as a vote where the sheet spacing is similar to the spacing of the calibration pairs and fails where it is not (single-vote accuracy 23% to 42% for k = 1 to 4 on the segment-label pairs below, 85% on the annotations). It is therefore a vote that depends on the pair length.

The votes of the annotation rows were computed at native L2 resolution per downloaded region; the votes of the segment-label pairs and the generated constraints are recomputed on the 2x down-sampled box arrays (`src/certlib.py`), with confidence taken from the length of the averaged vector. The annotation-trained model applied to these inputs gave the precision reported below, which is the evidence that the shift is tolerable for the Paris 4 box. One detail of the feature code: a cos peak count of -1 (no peak) is mapped to 1 by an absolute value in the annotation path that trains the gradient-boosting certifier (2 of 17,425 annotation pairs have this value) and is kept as -1 in the deployed path (segment-label pairs, generated constraints, `src/certnet.py`), where it never equals the candidate count (0.7% of the segment-label pairs, 167 of 25,000 in one training file). The effect is small and has not been measured; making both paths take the absolute value is left to the code.

## Winding-count certificates on pairs drawn from the dense truth (W2 slab z 10500-11000)

The human relative-winding annotations have about 17 thousand pairs and a known annotation noise. To check the certificates on a larger sample with a second reference, `src/truth_pairs.py` draws straight lines through the slab (L2 z 10500-11000, y 2304-3840, x 3584-5120; segment-label field of the 77f5 truth, derived from the official segments), directions radial from the umbilicus with a normal 25 degree rotation and a z slope of +-0.35, rays up to 140 voxels long. The end points are two integer crossings of the truth phase (sheet centres), and the truth count k is the difference of the crossing numbers; the pair length has median 26, 99th percentile 101 and maximum 131 voxels. Lines whose truth phase is invalid or not monotone are not used, so the sample is the cleaner part of the slab, and the segment-label field cannot separate sheets closer than about 5 voxels. The slab is held out in z only: the network was trained on z 10000-10380 in the same y and x columns with labels from the same official segments, and the lower half of the slab (z 10500-10750) is the slab on which the model, the ensemble choice and the 0.27 gate were selected. The votes are computed only from the box A prediction arrays with the same code (`src/certlib.py`) as the generated constraints.

The certifier is the one trained on the human relative-winding annotations only (5,943 pairs of 1 to 4 windings, 227 collections, annotation regions that touch the truth box and the training z range removed), the grad_mag slopes are fitted on all annotation pairs and the threshold is fixed on the out-of-fold predictions made with these slopes (overall precision 98 / 99 / 99.5%), then applied unchanged to 4000 segment-label pairs (k = 1, 2, 3, 4: 2841, 707, 309, 143). `src/truth_certify.py` (log `truth_certify_3.log`, EXCL=1 EXCL_BOX=1 on the complete annotation rows).

## A learned winding counter and a combined certifier (development results)

The gradient-boosting certifier above sees a handful of summary votes per pair. The winding-count network sees the whole profile of the segment between the two points: 14 channels sampled every L2 voxel for up to 255 voxels (CT, surface prediction, v6 phase vector and confidence, frozen_8c phase vector, v6 phase on two lines 3 voxels to each side, lasagna cos and grad_mag), and outputs a distribution over the count (0 to 7, and 8 or more). Code: `src/profiles_truth.py` (training pairs), `src/counter_net.py` (a residual 1D convolutional network with dilations 1 to 16, masked normalisation, attention and mean/max pooling, 0.75 M parameters), `src/counter_train.py`.

Training data are pairs from the W2 segment-label field only; no human annotation is used. The lines are radial rays rotated by a normal angle, with a z slope of +-0.35, up to 260 voxels long; the end points sit at integer crossings of the truth phase with a random offset (human points are about 0.1 turn off the sheet centre); the count k is uniform over the feasible counts up to 10, and 4% of the pairs have two points on one sheet. There are two training mixes: 130,000 pairs (T1 to T4) with a normal 25 degree rotation and an end point offset of up to 0.2 turn, and 120,000 pairs (T5, T6) with a 45 degree rotation and an offset of up to 0.35 turn. Validation and test use the 25 degree and 0.2 turn mix, the easier one. With the larger offset, the label (the difference of the crossing numbers) cannot always be obtained by rounding the phase difference of the two end points: in a sample of 1,500 pairs of T5 this holds for 10.5% of the pairs (T1: 0.3%), which is about 5% of the whole training set. These labels carry noise that the network cannot remove, and its effect on the confidence has not been tested separately (the first variant, trained on T1 to T4 only, is the available comparison). The metadata string of the profile files did not record the rotation angle; the 45 degrees of T5 and T6 are inferred from the |cos| between line and radial direction (median 0.859, against 0.94 to 0.95 for the other files). The 250,000 pairs start in layers 10500 to 10750 and x columns 0 to 1100 of the slab. Validation (10,000 pairs, used for early stopping, the temperature and the thresholds of the truth-pair table) and test (10,000 pairs) use other rays that start in layers 10800 to 11000. The 50-layer buffer is a gap between start layers only: a ray with a z slope of 0.35 and a length of 260 voxels spans about 90 layers in z, so a training ray and a test ray can reach about 40 layers beyond the start layers of the other set and pass through the same voxels. The lasagna channels are set to zero in 30% of the training samples; reversal of the line, cropping of the ends and a small phase rotation are used as augmentation. The reversal flips the sample order of the profile, which moves the fractional last step to the start, so it differs from a profile computed from the other end by a shift of 0 to 1 voxel; no effect on the 97% validation accuracy was seen. Training takes about 27 minutes on one RTX 4080 laptop GPU.

## Generated constraints from predictions only (W2 slab, scored against the segment-label field)

`src/autoladder2.py` builds constraints without any human point. Along a line it unwraps the v6 phase and takes the integer crossings as candidate sheet centres; each crossing is moved to the nearest local maximum of the surface prediction within 4 voxels, and a crossing is kept when the v6 confidence there is at least PCONF. From the current end point i the next kept points i+1, i+2, i+3 are tried in order; the first link for which the learned certifier (trained on human relative-winding annotations only, threshold at annotation-side overall precision 99%) gives a probability above the threshold, with a certified count c from 1 to 4, is accepted and the wind number grows by c. If none of the three is accepted the constraint ends. `src/autoladder_prod.py` does the same anywhere in box A and writes the community point-collection format (`winding_is_absolute: false`, points `p = [x, y, z]` in L2 voxels and `wind_a`, plus `link_prob` and `v6_conf`).

Scoring (`src/autoladder2_eval.py`; the truth never enters the generator): a link is correct when both end points lie within TOL turns of a truth sheet centre of the segment-label field and the truth sheet count between them equals the certified count c. TOL = 0.15 turn (about 3 voxels) was fixed as the primary criterion before the final reading; 0.30 turn (about 6 voxels) is reported too, and the preregistered failure line (98% at TOL 0.30) is written on it. For reference, the 38 human annotation points that fall inside the valid truth of the slab are within 0.15 turn of the label-field sheet centre in 63% of the cases (95% interval about 47% to 77%) and within 0.30 turn in 87%. This counts single points, while a link needs two end points within tolerance, and the segment-label field is the kind of target the network was trained on, so the comparison does not show that the automatic points are more accurate than the human points. The final reading used 1000 lines that start in z 10750-11000 (5574 truth sheets crossed), the same lines for the three PCONF values; with a z slope up to 0.35 over 300 voxels the development and final lines can overlap in z. ## A learned bridge-edge detector (exploration, not adopted)

The surfaces are limited by bridges between touching sheets (the previous section). `a2/bridge_clf.py` trained a gradient-boosting classifier for the bridge edges of the level-set pieces of box A (860 pieces, 76.2 million mesh edges, 1.68% bridge edges by the truth purity rule) from 38 features that come only from the network side (confidence, phase gradient and vortex distance, lasagna, CT and surface-prediction statistics, width of the surface prediction along the normal, mesh geometry, geodesic distance to the piece boundary, piece-level quantities); the truth is used for labels and scoring only. Cross-validation grouped by piece: AUC 0.72 (0.71 to 0.75 per fold), average precision 0.035 against a base rate of 0.017. Cutting the edges with the highest scores keeps a purity of 91.05% at 56.8 cm2 against the preregistered line of 93% at 55 cm2 or more (baseline 89.58% at 60.9 cm2), the same as a plain confidence gate at the same area (difference -0.4 to +0.1 points), and the patch check switch rate is unchanged (53/2945 = 1.8%, baseline 52/2971). The Cs thresholds were chosen after looking at that agreement (two settings were tried: v6 confidence 0.8 gave 96.99%, 0.85 gave 97.34%); the held-out human relative-winding annotations were not used for the choice, but the truth slab and the evaluation window overlap. The arms differ in the number of pairs that can be assigned (31 to 41), the window holds only 41 held-out pairs, and there is one window. This is an exploratory reading and is not a comparison with PCU, whose bands, protocol and fitter hardware differ.

## Retraining the winding-count network (negative result)

The missed-sheet errors of the combined certifier (a multi-winding pair certified with fewer windings than the truth: 21 of 3,882 certified pairs at the 99% target) are confident: requiring the winding-count network's probability for fewer windings to be at most 0.002 removes 9 of them and lowers the coverage from 65.3% to 50.5%. Two retrained versions were tried on 2026-10-08 with the same folds, combined certifier and protocol as above (`src/counter_train.py` options `--skip`, `src/profiles_truth.py` environment ANG, DZ, KUNIF, JIT). Both add 120,000 pairs (`T7`, `T8`: ray direction scatter 70 degrees instead of 25, z slope up to 0.6, endpoint jitter 0.35 turn, winding count uniform) to the 250,000 pairs of the current model.
